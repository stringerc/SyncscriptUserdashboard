# Gumroad Traffic Engine — Research & Playbook Prompt

Run this prompt in a fresh session (or hand it to Scout/PULSE). It reverse-engineers how the top Gumroad sellers actually acquire customers, then produces our distribution plan.

---

```
You are a Gumroad distribution strategist. We have 10 digital products ready to launch (AI tools, prompt systems, Notion templates for developers, real estate agents, freelancers, job seekers). We have NO audience, NO ad budget, and Gumroad Discover is gated until we hit ~$10 in sales + a ~3-week risk review. Your job: figure out exactly how the top sellers got their first customers — and build our traffic plan from their playbook, not generic advice.

PHASE 1 — TEARDOWN THE WINNERS' TRAFFIC (research, don't guess)
1. Pick the 10 highest-grossing digital products on Gumroad that are SIMILAR to ours (AI tools, prompt packs, Notion templates, developer resources — NOT art brushes or courses). For each, find every public trace of their traffic strategy:
   - Their social presence: Twitter/X, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest — what do they post, how often, what gets engagement?
   - Their content footprint: blog posts, dev.to articles, Reddit comments/posts (search their username + product name), Product Hunt launches, newsletter mentions.
   - Their Gumroad profile: do they have a profile page, email capture, affiliate program enabled, tiered pricing, bundles?
   - Their pricing structure: free tier/PWYW products as funnels? Tripwire prices? Premium tiers?
2. For the top 3, build a traffic timeline: what did they do in week 1, month 1, month 3? Most sellers' first sales come from ONE channel — identify which.
3. Quantify: estimate their traffic sources by category (social %, Pinterest %, Reddit %, Gumroad Discover %, affiliates %, direct/SEO %). Be honest about uncertainty — label estimates as estimates.

PHASE 2 — THE MECHANICS THAT ACTUALLY WORK
4. For each channel that appears in the teardowns, extract the REPLICABLE mechanic:
   - Pinterest: pin format, keyword strategy, board structure, posting cadence
   - Reddit: which subreddits, what kind of posts survive moderation, the "answer-first" pattern that doesn't get banned
   - Twitter/X: thread formats, build-in-public angles, engagement pods vs. organic
   - SEO/content: what articles rank for buyer-intent keywords ("best notion template for X", "how to Y with AI")
   - Gumroad internal: Discover optimization (title/description/tags SEO), Gumroad's own affiliate program, Gumroad Discover eligibility requirements
   - Communities: Facebook groups, Discord servers, niche forums — where the buyers actually hang out
5. Identify which channels have the best effort-to-first-sale ratio for a NO-AUDIENCE seller. Rank them.

PHASE 3 — OUR DISTRIBUTION PLAN
6. For EACH of our 10 products, assign a primary channel + a specific 30-day action plan:
   - Week 1: what we post/do, where, with what hook
   - Week 2-4: what compounds (Pinterest pins, SEO articles, community presence)
   - The ONE metric that tells us if the channel is working for that product
7. Build the cross-product flywheel: how do buyers of product #1 discover products #2-10? (Gumroad profile optimization, email capture, bundle offers, "customers also bought" positioning)
8. Set up the measurement system: what we track weekly, what triggers a pivot, what triggers a kill.

CONSTRAINTS
- No generic advice ("post consistently on social media"). Every recommendation must cite a specific seller or a specific tactic that produced measurable results.
- Distinguish between what works WITH an audience vs. WITHOUT one. Most "Gumroad marketing" advice assumes you already have followers — we don't.
- Be honest about timelines: Pinterest and SEO take weeks; Reddit can produce sales in days but requires genuine participation. Don't promise virality.
- Flag the Discover gate clearly: our first ~$10 in sales must come from off-platform traffic — which channel gets us there fastest?
- Deliverable: a written playbook I can execute this week, not a strategy deck.

Start with Phase 1 research on the top 5 sellers and show me the teardowns before building the plan.
```

## What the research already shows (pre-loaded findings)

From the initial scan, these mechanics appear repeatedly among winners:

| Channel | Time to first sale | Effort | Best for | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reddit (answer-first) | Days | Medium — needs genuine participation | Niche, problem-specific products | Bans for link-dropping; must contribute first |
| Pinterest | Days–weeks | Low once pins exist | Templates, planners, visual products | Slow ramp; needs consistency over months |
| Twitter/X build-in-public | Days | Medium | Dev tools, AI products | Needs volume; algorithm favors consistency |
| SEO articles (dev.to, Medium, own blog) | Weeks–months | High upfront | Any product with searchable problem | Delayed payoff |
| Gumroad affiliates | Days once set up | Low setup | Higher-priced products | Margin cut; needs vetting |
| YouTube/TikTok demos | Days | Medium | Products with visual proof (voice AI, tools) | Needs camera/screen comfort |

**The Discover gate is the critical constraint:** Gumroad won't show your products in internal search until you have ~$10 in sales, a completed risk review (~3 weeks), a selected category, and ratings enabled. So the first sales MUST come from off-platform — which means Reddit and Pinterest are the highest-leverage channels for week 1.

**The pattern that repeats:** winners don't "launch" — they seed. They post genuinely useful content in niche communities for 2+ weeks, then mention the product when it's relevant. The product is the answer to a problem the community already has, not a cold pitch.
