# AI Listing Hook Pack — 50 Scroll-Stopping Opening Lines

The first line of your listing description decides whether a buyer reads the second one. These 50 hooks are written to stop the scroll, create an open loop, and make the reader need the next sentence.

**How to use:** Pick a hook that matches the property and buyer type. Fill in [BRACKETS]. Paste it as the opening line of your listing description, social post, or email — then let the body of your description (or the full prompt library in the Real Estate AI Listing Kit) do the rest.

**The rule:** A hook should create a question the buyer needs answered. If your first line could describe any house on any street, it hooks nobody.

---

## Category 1 — The Lifestyle Hook (emotional pull)

1. Every morning in this [neighborhood] home starts with [specific feature — morning light through bay windows / coffee on the deck overlooking the park].
2. [Number] years from now, your kids will measure their childhood in summers at this [feature].
3. This isn't a house with a yard. It's a yard that happens to have a house on it.
4. The kind of home where Sunday mornings don't have an alarm clock.
5. If your idea of a good weekend is [activity the property enables], keep reading.
6. Some houses you tour. This one you move into mentally on the driveway.
7. [Neighborhood] doesn't have listings like this twice a decade. This is one of them.
8. The difference between this home and the last one you saw? This one feels like yours already.
9. There's a reason the buyers who tour this place don't want to leave.
10. You know that feeling when you walk into a space and your shoulders drop? That's this house.

## Category 2 — The Specificity Hook (concrete detail = credibility)

11. Built in [year], updated in [year] — the kitchen you see in the photos is the kitchen you'll cook in on day one.
12. [Sqft] square feet, [beds] bedrooms, one very specific reason the third bedroom matters: [feature].
13. The roof was replaced in [year]. The HVAC is [age]. The [feature] is [age]. You're buying a home, not a project.
14. [Number] blocks to [landmark]. [Number] minutes to [commute]. We timed it — here's the actual route.
15. That [feature] isn't stock. It's [custom detail — reclaimed beams / a barn door the seller built / original 1920s hardwood under the carpet].
16. The listing photos don't capture it, but the [room] gets [specific light quality] from [time] until [time].
17. Taxes are $[amount]/year. HOA is $[amount]/month. No surprises hiding in the fine print.
18. [Feature] was installed [when] and comes with [warranty/receipt/proof]. We have the paperwork.
19. Every window in the primary [direction] bedroom frames the same view — and it changes with the seasons.
20. The sellers are leaving behind [list: the patio furniture / the shed / the wine fridge]. Details in the disclosures.

## Category 3 — The Curiosity / Open-Loop Hook

21. The most photographed room in this house isn't the kitchen. It's [unexpected space]. Here's why.
22. Three buyers toured this property last weekend. All three asked the same question. It's a good one.
23. There's one thing about this listing that the photos can't show you — and it might be the reason you buy.
24. The sellers will tell you exactly why they're leaving. It's not what you'd guess.
25. Everyone walks past [hidden feature] on the tour. Almost nobody notices it until the second visit.
26. This listing is priced [below/at/above] market — here's the honest math on why that number is right.
27. The neighborhood has a secret. It's been here for [time] and most residents don't know it exists.
28. You can have the big [feature] or the small [feature]. In this house, you don't have to choose.
29. The previous owner did one thing to this place that tripled its feel — and it's still there.
30. There's a reason this property sits on the market while the one around the corner sold in four days. Read on.

## Category 4 — The Neighborhood Hook (location as the product)

31. [Neighborhood] is having a moment — [new restaurant / light rail line / school rating jump] — and this house puts you in the middle of it.
32. You're [X] minutes from [downtown/airport/beach] without [traffic/train noise/the commute]. We checked both directions.
33. The street this sits on has [character detail — mature trees / zero through-traffic / neighbors who actually know each other].
34. Walk to [specific amenity] in [time]. Drive to [amenity] in [time]. The map doesn't lie — we drove it.
35. [School name] is [rating] minutes away. The district boundary runs down [street] — this house is on the right side of it.
36. This is the [neighborhood] home that makes the listing across town look like a compromise.
37. [Local landmark] is your new backyard view. At night, the [detail] lights up — it's better than the photos.
38. The neighborhood association newsletter features this street every spring. There's a reason.
39. You're not buying a house in [city]. You're buying a Tuesday — [describe the actual weekly rhythm the location enables].
40. [Neighborhood]'s secret isn't a secret anymore — prices are up [X]% in [timeframe] — but this pocket still sleeps under the radar.

## Category 5 — The Proof / Social Hook (credibility openers)

41. [Number] families have made this house their home before you. The bones are good — the sellers just want a change.
42. [Agent/team name] has sold [number] homes in [neighborhood]. This is the one we'd buy ourselves.
43. The inspection came back clean — [list 1-2 specifics: no deferred maintenance, new roof, updated electrical]. The report is available.
44. Price reduced [amount] last week. The seller is motivated, the house is priced right now, and offers are being reviewed daily.
45. This property has been on the market [time] — not because anything's wrong, but because the right buyer hasn't walked through yet.
46. We're not asking you to imagine what this could be. The sellers did the work — here's what's already done.
47. Every comparable on this street sold within [timeframe] for [price range]. This one is priced to move.
48. The photos are real. The video tour is unedited. What you see is what you get — we don't do the filter thing.
49. [Number] showings this weekend alone. If you've been thinking about it, this is the weekend to see it.
50. The seller's disclosure says "well-maintained." Here's what that actually means in this house: [specific proof points].

## Quick formulas (when none of the 50 fit)

- **The contrast:** "The [feature everyone expects] is there. The [unexpected detail] is what sells it."
- **The time anchor:** "On [specific date/season], this house does something no other house on the street can."
- **The honest one:** "We're going to be straight with you about this property — including the one thing most listings won't mention."
- **The buyer mirror:** "If you've ever [specific frustration with current living situation], keep reading."

## What a hook is NOT

- "Welcome to [address]" — nobody is welcomed; they're sold.
- "Stunning" / "beautiful" / "charming" — adjectives without evidence are noise.
- A hook that could sit on any listing in any city — specificity is the whole game.
- Clickbait that the body doesn't pay off — the hook opens a loop; the description closes it.
