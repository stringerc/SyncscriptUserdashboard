"Let me tell you what I did. On May 1, 2026 — a Friday afternoon at 4:48 PM, twelve minutes before the courthouse closed — I walked into the Spalding County State Court Clerk's Office and tried to file a flash drive. A flash drive containing video evidence for my own criminal case."

"The clerk's office told me they couldn't accept physical media. The court has an e-file-only mandate. I was still represented by counsel at the time — my attorneys hadn't formally withdrawn yet — so the clerk also said I shouldn't be filing directly. The deputy clerk told me to come back Monday when the elected clerk and the chief judge would be present."

"I didn't come back Monday. Instead, at 4:48 PM, I handwritten a document that said 'Video Evidence Logged with Clerk' and I left both that document and the flash drive on the counter. Over the deputy clerk's objections. And I walked out."

*Pause. Let the audience judge this.*

"The court says my tone was aggressive. That my volume was unacceptable. That other clerks stopped working to watch. That the disruption affected 'the orderly operations of the Court, Judge Shepherd, Court Services, and the Clerk's Office.' That the court expended 'many hours' reviewing the incident between May 1 and May 28."

"And at my hearing on May 28, the court says I 'accepted absolutely no responsibility' and 'aggressively cross-examined the Deputy Clerk.'"

**YOU:** "Now let me tell you what the order does NOT say. It doesn't say I threatened anyone. It doesn't say I touched anyone. It doesn't say I damaged property. It doesn't say I obstructed a judicial proceeding. It doesn't say I interfered with a court order."

"What it says is: a pro se defendant, twelve minutes before closing, tried to file a flash drive containing evidence for his own criminal case, left it on the counter when told to come back another day, and walked out."

"For that — for leaving a flash drive on a counter — I was found guilty beyond a reasonable doubt of indirect criminal contempt of court."

*Hold on your face. Let the audience sit with the disproportionality.*

**YOU:** "Now here's where it gets worse."

"Order number two — same day, same judge, same filing timestamp — my bond is revoked. In full. I am ordered to appear in court on June 11, 2026 at 9:00 AM. And upon entering the courtroom, I will be taken into custody by the Spalding County Sheriff's Office and immediately transported to the Spalding County Detention Center."

"I will be held without bond until resolution of this matter or further order of the Court."

**VISUAL:** The order text appears on screen, highlighted:

**YOU:** "Let me read you the reasons the judge gave for revoking my bond."

- License suspension from a failure to appear in another court

- My post-hearing file sharing conduct

"And: 'Singular willful and intentional refusal to follow even the simplest of instructions.' The instructions in question were: come back Monday to file your flash drive. I left it on the counter instead. That refusal — leaving a flash drive on a counter instead of coming back on Monday — is now the basis for revoking my bond and ordering me into custody."

**YOU:** "The judge also wrote something about me that I think is important for you to hear. He said: 'Defendant is a technologically sophisticated and well-organized individual who appears at each court date dressed appropriately, with a large 3-ring notebook and a Apple computer before him.'"

"Technologically sophisticated. Well-organized. Dressed appropriately. The judge is describing competence. And he's using it against me. The same way Trant weaponized my AI use in her contempt motion. The same way the court used my phantom citations to justify a $20,000 fee award. **YOU:** "There's one more thing in this order that I need you to hear. The judge acknowledged — on the record, in writing — that my statutory speedy trial demand 'REMAINS IN FULL FORCE AND EFFECT.' "So the court is saying: your speedy trial demand is valid. But if you miss one court appearance, it self-destructs. And they're telling me this while ordering me to appear in a courtroom where I will be taken into custody."

"I have five pending cases. A custody hearing. A criminal trial on August 17. A discretionary appeal pending in the Court of Appeals. Active Board complaints. A malpractice counterclaim. And the court just ordered me into a detention center three days from now."

**YOU:** "This is not a custody dispute anymore. This is not a criminal case anymore. A judge just ordered a pro se defendant — a father — into custody for leaving a flash drive on a clerk's counter.
